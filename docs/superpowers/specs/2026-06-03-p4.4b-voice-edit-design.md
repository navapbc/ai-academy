# P4.4b — "Voice-edit" Lab (cell 2.6)

**Phase:** P4.4b — *A two-phase "AI-draft → AI-off revision" exercise on cell 2.6. The learner generates an AI first draft live, then revises it in their own voice; the revision is scored by the existing P4.2 LLM-judge against a rubric, shown in place. Practice, not a gate.*
**Depends on:** P4.3b (#48 — the `{brief, sections[]}` grade judge, `requestLlmGrade`/`saveGrade`/`recordLabSubmission`, the shared `GradeResultCard`), P4.4a (#51 — the `SourcedFreeTextLab`/`Synthesis` save→grade→`GradeResultCard` flow), P1.2 (`streamChat` + the model selector), P3.6/P3.7 (the render-above-the-quiz exercise pattern).
**Date:** 2026-06-03
**Branch:** `feat/p4.4b-voice-edit`

## 1. Goal

Add a new interactive exercise — **Voice-edit** — that teaches the central point of cell 2.6
("AI for writing tasks"): writing is the highest-volume way people use AI, and the value is in
**what you do with the draft**. AI first drafts tend to flatten specifics, over-generalize, and
adopt a generic voice; the editor's job is to restore the concrete detail, fix reading level and
tone, and verify every fact. The exercise makes the learner do exactly that:

1. read a dense, jargon-heavy **source** + a **writing brief**,
2. generate an **AI first draft** live (Claude via `streamChat`),
3. **revise that draft "AI off"** in their own voice — restoring specifics the draft dropped or
   generalized, fixing reading level + tone,
4. submit the revision, which the **existing P4.2 LLM-judge** anchor-scores against a rubric
   (graded on the **revision**, relative to the source + the draft), rendered in place via
   `GradeResultCard`.

This is **graded practice that records a `lab_submission` but does NOT gate completion**. Cell 2.6
is `type:'content'` with an inline quiz; the **quiz stays the completion gate** (same pattern as
the #48 critique and #51 synthesis — render above the quiz, never call `onComplete`).

Wired on one cell:
- **2.6 "AI for writing tasks"** — turn a dense internal **child-care-subsidy redetermination case
  note** into a short, plain-language notice for the parent who will read it. The source carries
  must-preserve specifics (a required form, two pay stubs, a deadline, a copay change, a
  consequence). A good revision keeps every one of them at a sixth-grade reading level with one
  clear next step.

**Out of scope:** champion-review UI/RLS (P5.1, unchanged); retrofitting other exercises; the
judge's prompt/parser (`GRADE_SYSTEM_PROMPT`, `parseVerdict` — untouched); any change to the
`chat` or `grade` functions or the quiz gate. No regression to the 2.1 lab, the 2.2/2.3 critique,
or the 2.7 synthesis introduced in #48/#51.

## 2. Why a seeded source makes it reliably teachable

The AI draft is generated **live** and is non-deterministic, so the exercise cannot depend on the
draft being bad. Instead the **source** is seeded with specific, must-preserve details (a figure,
a date/deadline, a required document, a consequence) and the constraints live in the **brief**.
The learner's job — and the rubric — is to verify the draft against the source and restore/fix what
is needed. That holds regardless of how good the live draft happens to be: if the draft already
kept everything, a faithful revision still scores; if the draft flattened a specific, the revision
that restores it is what the rubric rewards. The source is **synthetic** (authored here), so there
is no external-fact risk; figures are marked "(illustrative)". See §6 for the full answer key.

## 3. Context (current state, post-#48/#51)

- **The P4.2 judge** (`supabase/functions/grade/{verdict.ts,index.ts}`) is non-streaming and, as
  generalized in #48, takes `GradeSubmission = { brief: string; sections: { label; text }[] }`.
  `buildGradeUserMessage` renders the brief then each `LABEL:`\n`text` verbatim. A voice-edit fits
  with **no judge change**: pass `sections: [{label:'Source', …}, {label:'AI first draft', …},
  {label:"The learner's revision", …}]` (three sections instead of two).
- **The client grading path** (`src/lib/grading.ts requestLlmGrade`, `src/lib/progress.ts
  recordLabSubmission`→id + `saveGrade`) is reused as-is. `recordLabSubmission`'s `transcript` is
  free-form (`unknown`), so `{kind:'voice-edit', draft, revision, wordCount}` needs no schema
  change.
- **`streamChat`** (`src/lib/llm.ts`) streams Claude through the `chat` Edge Function with an
  `AbortSignal`. **`src/components/Lab.tsx`** is the reference for the streaming-generate flow: the
  model selector (`CLAUDE_MODELS`/`DEFAULT_MODEL_ID`), the `abortRef` + `useEffect(() =>
  abortRef.current?.abort())` unmount cleanup, the per-run `new AbortController()`, the
  `aria-live` output region. Voice-edit's phase 1 mirrors this exactly.
- **`src/components/GradeResultCard.tsx`** (#48) is the anchor-scored card (`#grade-result`,
  `role="status"`/`aria-live`, "Anchor-scored feedback … Provisional — pending review"). **Reused
  as-is, no edit.**
- **`SourcedFreeTextLab`/`Critique`/`Synthesis`** (#48/#51) are the save→grade→card reference: Save
  → `recordLabSubmission({transcript:{kind,…},status:'submitted'})` → `requestLlmGrade({rubric,
  submission})` → `saveGrade(id, result, 'reviewable')` → `<GradeResultCard>`; no `onComplete`; a
  `@ts-expect-error` test asserts `onComplete` is not accepted.
- **Content lives in two places:** lesson/quiz come from `curriculum-content.json` (already seeded
  for 2.6 — "AI for writing tasks", 3 quiz questions). **Lab configs come from migrations.** Cell
  2.6 has body+quiz but **no `lab_config_json`** yet.
- **The 2.6 lesson's worked example** is a *14-page eligibility policy* turned into a 150-word
  notice where the draft drops a *form name + a June 30 date*. Reusing that would hand the learner
  the answer — so the exercise uses a **different** scenario (a child-care-subsidy redetermination)
  with **different** specifics (Form CCS-9, two pay stubs, an August 15 deadline, a copay change,
  the subsidy-ends consequence).

## 5. Types, component, dispatch

`src/types.ts` — additive union member (other members untouched):

```ts
/**
 * 2.6 voice-edit (P4.4b): the learner reads a dense source + a writing brief,
 * generates an AI FIRST DRAFT live (streamChat), then revises it "AI off" in
 * their own voice — restoring specifics the draft dropped/generalized and fixing
 * reading level + tone. The revision is graded by the P4.2 LLM-judge against
 * `rubric` (sections: Source + AI first draft + the revision); the anchor-scored
 * result renders in place. Graded PRACTICE that records a lab_submissions row
 * (`transcript.kind:'voice-edit'`) — it does NOT gate completion (the inline quiz
 * does), so the component takes no onComplete prop.
 */
export interface VoiceEditConfig {
  kind: 'voice-edit';
  title?: string;
  subtitle?: string;
  /** The dense source to rewrite — contains the must-preserve specifics. */
  source: { label: string; bodyMd: string };
  /** The writing task + constraints (reading level, length, tone, "preserve every specific", one next step). */
  brief: { instruction: string; constraints?: string[] };
  /** P4.2 anchors. */
  rubric: GradingRubric;
}
// added to: export type LabConfig = … | VoiceEditConfig;
```

## 6. Content — the source, brief, rubric, and the ANSWER KEY (synthetic)

> Authoring bar (`docs/content-guide.md`): no AI-isms; realistic civic-tech context. The source is
> **synthetic** (an authored internal case note) — there is no external-fact risk — but it is
> internally consistent and every must-preserve specific is genuinely present so the rubric is
> fair. The scenario (a child-care-subsidy redetermination) and the specifics intentionally
> **differ** from the 2.6 lesson's worked example (a 14-page eligibility policy turned into a notice
> that drops a form name + a June 30 date), so the exercise doesn't hand the learner the answer.
> Figures are marked "(illustrative)".

**Source (`source.label` + `source.bodyMd`, dense internal case note):** a Child Care Subsidy (CCS)
annual-redetermination case note. The household's 12-month eligibility period ends **August 31,
2026**; continued help requires a completed redetermination before it closes. Specifics:
- **Required action:** submit a completed **Form CCS-9 (Redetermination)** and the household's
  **two most recent pay stubs** (to verify earned income).
- **Deadline:** received **no later than August 15, 2026** (fifteen business days before the period
  closes).
- **Copay change:** updated income moves the household to a higher tier — if approved, the
  **monthly family copay rises from $45 to $72 (illustrative)**, effective **September 1, 2026**.
- **Consequence of non-response:** if the form + pay stubs are not received by August 15, 2026, the
  **subsidy ends August 31, 2026**; the family must reapply, a new determination can take **up to
  30 days**, and the provider is not guaranteed to hold the child's slot.

**Brief (`brief.instruction`):** "A caseworker handed you this internal case note and needs a notice
to send the family today. Generate an AI first draft, then revise it — AI off — for the parent who
will actually read it. AI drafts tend to flatten the specifics into vague reassurance and adopt a
generic voice; your job is to keep every concrete detail, write it plainly and warmly, and end with
one clear next step."

**Brief constraints (`brief.constraints`):**
- "About 150 words or fewer."
- "Sixth-grade reading level — short sentences, common words, active voice, and ‘you.’ Don't make the reader decode jargon like ‘redetermination’ or ‘eligibility period.’"
- "Warm, respectful tone — this is a family, not a file."
- "Preserve every specific: Form CCS-9, the two most recent pay stubs, the August 15, 2026 deadline, the new $72 monthly copay starting September 1, 2026, and the consequence that the subsidy ends August 31, 2026 if nothing is returned."
- "End with one clear next step."

**Rubric anchors (4 × 0–2):**

| id | label | description (what a good revision does) |
|----|-------|------------------------------------------|
| `preserve-specifics` | Keep every specific | Keeps every concrete detail from the source — Form CCS-9, the two most recent pay stubs, the August 15, 2026 deadline, the new $72 monthly copay effective September 1, 2026, and the consequence that the subsidy ends August 31, 2026 — rather than dropping any or softening them into vague phrases like "submit your documents soon." |
| `plain-language` | Hit the plain-language target | Reads at roughly a sixth-grade level — short sentences, common words, active voice, "you" — and explains or avoids program jargon (e.g. "redetermination," "eligibility period") so a parent can act without decoding it. |
| `tone-and-next-step` | Right tone, one next step | Reads in a warm, respectful tone appropriate for a family and ends with one clear next step (return Form CCS-9 and the two pay stubs by August 15, 2026), rather than a vague "contact us" or a list of competing actions. |
| `improves-on-draft` | Improve on the draft | The revision genuinely improves on the AI first draft — it restores specifics the draft dropped or generalized and fixes tone/reading-level problems — rather than submitting the draft essentially verbatim. |

**ANSWER KEY — what a good revision MUST do:**

1. **Preserve all five specifics**, exactly: (a) **Form CCS-9 (Redetermination)** by name; (b) the
   **two most recent pay stubs**; (c) the **August 15, 2026** deadline; (d) the **new $72 monthly
   copay** (up from $45), **effective September 1, 2026**; (e) the consequence — **subsidy ends
   August 31, 2026** if nothing is returned (reapply, up to 30 days, slot not guaranteed). Dropping
   or vaguely generalizing any of these (e.g. "send your paperwork soon") loses
   `preserve-specifics`.
2. **Plain language, ~6th grade, ≤~150 words.** Short sentences, common words, active voice, "you";
   no undefined jargon ("redetermination," "eligibility period," "copay tier").
3. **Warm, respectful tone + exactly one clear next step** — "Return Form CCS-9 and your two most
   recent pay stubs by August 15, 2026" — not a vague "contact us" or several competing asks.
4. **The revision improves on the draft** — restores/corrects rather than submitting it verbatim.

**Target output (illustrative — not shown to the learner):** a ~120-word notice at ~6th grade, e.g.
"Your child care help is up for its yearly review. To keep it, send us two things by **August 15,
2026**: a completed **Form CCS-9** and your **two most recent pay stubs**. If we get them in time
and you still qualify, your monthly payment will change from **$45 to $72** starting **September 1,
2026**. If we don't get them by August 15, your child care help will **end on August 31, 2026**, and
you'd have to apply again — which can take up to 30 days, and your child's spot may not be held.
**Next step: return Form CCS-9 and your two pay stubs by August 15, 2026.** Questions? We're here to
help."

**Framing grounded in the lesson's own sources** (no new external claims): writing is the
highest-volume AI use and "where it goes bland the fastest"; the editor stays "the editor of last
resort," and "every fact that reaches a claimant is yours to verify"; federal plain-language
guidance calls for short sentences, common words, active voice, and "you." (Lesson 2.6 "AI for
writing tasks"; Anthropic 4D AI Fluency framework; Plain Writing Act of 2010 / plain-language
guidance.)
