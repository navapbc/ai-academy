# P4.3c — "Confidence-calibration" Lab (cell 2.8)

**Phase:** P4.3c — *A calibration exercise on cell 2.8: the learner sets a verification posture for several outputs from the SAME AI tool, then sees an over-/under-reliance summary. AUTO-GRADED against an answer key (no LLM). Practice, not a gate.*
**Depends on:** P4.3a (#49 — the `output-audit` auto-graded, render-above-the-quiz pattern: pure grader + `.test.ts`, `recordLabSubmission`, the `@ts-expect-error` no-`onComplete` guard). **Independent of P4.2/P4.3b** — no LLM judge, no shared grading code.
**Date:** 2026-06-03
**Branch:** `feat/p4.3c-confidence-calibration`

## 1. Goal

Add a new interactive exercise — **Calibration** — where the learner sees several outputs from
the **SAME AI tool** across different task types/stakes and, for each, picks the right
**verification posture** on an ordered scale (most-trusting → least-trusting). It is
**AUTO-GRADED against a deterministic answer key — no LLM call** — and, the part that makes it
a *calibration* exercise rather than another classifier, it produces an **over-/under-reliance
summary**: where the learner trusted high-risk output too readily (over-reliance) and where
they over-verified safe output (under-reliance).

This is **graded practice that records a `lab_submission` but does NOT gate completion**. Cell
2.8 is `type:'content'` with an inline quiz; the **quiz stays the completion gate** (same
P3.6/P3.7/P4.3a pattern — render above the quiz, never call `onComplete`).

Wired on one cell:
- **2.8 "Calibrated trust (avoiding over- and under-reliance)"** — six outputs from one tool,
  from "reformat a list you wrote" (use-as-is) to "compute an exact benefit figure for a
  determination notice" / "rule on a novel eligibility question with a citation" (verify-
  everything / don't-rely).

**Not the P3.5 scenario-sorter.** The sorter decides *whether / how* to involve AI in a task
(delegate/assist/human-only/refuse, graded for correctness). Calibration decides *how much to
trust/verify an output you've **already received***, and reports an over/under **direction-of-
miss** — a different axis and a different result shape.

**Out of scope:** the P4.2 LLM judge / `GradeResultCard` (untouched — deterministic); the
scenario-sorter; champion-review UI/RLS (P5.1); retrofitting other cells; matrix Stages 3–4;
any change to the quiz gate, the `chat`/`grade` functions, or other exercise components.

## 2. Context (current state)

- **The auto-graded exercise pattern** (`OutputAudit` (P4.3a), `DataClassifier`,
  `FailureSpotter`): a self-contained component that renders items, **auto-grades against a key
  on Submit** via a **pure, unit-tested grader**, reveals each item's `why`, records a
  `lab_submissions` row tagged by `transcript.kind`, and **does not accept `onComplete`**
  (a `@ts-expect-error` test proves it). `ModuleRenderer.renderExercise()` dispatches on
  `module.labConfig.kind`; the inline quiz renders after and gates.
- **`recordLabSubmission(userId, { labId, transcript, status })`** (`src/lib/progress.ts`) —
  append-only, RLS-owner-only.
- **Content lives in two places:** lesson/quiz from `curriculum-content.json`; lab configs from
  migrations. Cell 2.8 has body+quiz but **no `lab_config_json`** yet.
- **Cell 2.8 is Stage 2**, which is **locked until Stage 1a is complete** (`isModuleLocked`), so
  the E2E completes Stage 1a first (the `completeStage1a` helper, as `03-prompt-lab` does for 2.1).
- **The 2.8 lesson** frames calibrated trust as sitting between **over-reliance** (rubber-
  stamping) and **under-reliance** (reflexively rejecting), grounded in automation bias
  (Mosier & Skitka) and the **Jagged Frontier** (Dell'Acqua et al.). Its worked examples are
  *tighten an email, a meeting summary, and pulling the exact statute/citation for an appeal* —
  the exercise must not recite those.

## 3. Decisions

| # | Decision | Choice |
|---|----------|--------|
| D1 | New exercise vs. extend an existing one | **New `Calibration` component + `CalibrationConfig` union member.** Additive; no other component or config changes. |
| D2 | Grading | **Deterministic auto-grade, no LLM.** A pure grader `calibration.grade.ts` compares each pick's index on the ordered `scale` to the target index. |
| D3 | Completion semantics | **Practice, not a gate.** `Calibration` takes **no `onComplete`** (a `@ts-expect-error` test asserts this); the inline quiz remains the gate. Records `transcript.kind:'calibration'`, `status:'submitted'`. |
| D4 | The over/under axis | The `scale` is **ordered most-trusting (index 0) → least-trusting (last)**; the order is load-bearing. `gap = pickedIndex − targetIndex`: **negative = over-reliance** (too trusting), **positive = under-reliance** (too skeptical), **zero = calibrated**. |
| D5 | Score & tolerance | **Score = count of EXACTLY calibrated items (tolerance 0).** Exact match keeps the over/under signal unambiguous — a ±1 tolerance would blur the very direction-of-miss the exercise teaches. The summary surfaces the over/under counts regardless. |
| D6 | The summary card | Reveals per-item target + `why` AND a **summary card** naming the two failure modes in plain language, with counts. Per SME guidance, the card notes **over-reliance on high-stakes work is the costlier miss** (so learners see why item 6 ≠ item 1 in weight). |
| D7 | Content grounding | Items **cannot recite the 2.8 lesson's worked examples** (email-tighten / meeting-summary / exact-statute-for-appeal). Six items span the spectrum, all from one tool, SME-reviewed for defensible targets and low factual risk (no specific regulatory facts asserted as true). |
| D8 | Seed | One migration `20260603020000_seed_lab_config_2_8.sql`, `where cell_id='2.8' and lab_config_json is null`, `status='in_review'`, `version+1`. Idempotent. Timestamp `020000` clears main's latest AND P4.3a's `010000` AND P4.3b's `000000`, so no collision regardless of merge order. |

## 4. Types, grader, component, dispatch

`src/types.ts` — additive union member (other members untouched):

```ts
export interface CalibrationConfig {
  kind: 'calibration';
  intro?: string;
  // Ordered most-trusting (0) → least-trusting (last). Order is load-bearing.
  scale: { id: string; label: string; description?: string }[];
  items: { id: string; task: string; output?: string; target: string; why: string }[];
}
// added to: export type LabConfig = … | CalibrationConfig;
```

`src/components/calibration.grade.ts` — **pure** grader (no React, unit-tested):
`gradeCalibration(picks, { scale, items })` → per-item `{ id, pickedIndex, targetIndex, gap,
result: 'over'|'calibrated'|'under'|'unanswered' }`, a `summary { calibrated, over, under,
unanswered }`, `score` (= calibrated count), `total`, `allCalibrated`. A missing pick is
`unanswered` (gap `null`, counted in neither over nor under).

`src/components/exercises/Calibration.tsx` — renders `intro` and each item (`task` + optional
`output`) with one **ordered radiogroup** of postures. On Submit: `gradeCalibration`, reveal each
item's calibrated posture + `why` with an over/under/calibrated indicator, render the **over-/
under-reliance summary card**, and `recordLabSubmission(user.id, { labId, transcript:{
kind:'calibration', answers, score, maxScore, summary }, status:'submitted' })`. Accessible
(`role=radiogroup`/`radio`/`aria-checked`, `role=status aria-live=polite` per-item + summary,
`role=alert` save error, `sr-only` calibrated/miscalibrated). **No `onComplete` prop.** "Try
again" reset.

`ModuleRenderer.renderExercise()` — add `case 'calibration': return <Calibration
config={module.labConfig} labId={module.cellId} />;` (+ dispatch test case + mock).

## 5. The item set (cell 2.8)

Six outputs from one general-purpose AI assistant, civic-tech context, **different from the
lesson's worked examples**. Full text in the seed migration; answer key in §6. The set is
designed so the summary is meaningful: a clear use-as-is anchor (over-verifying it = under-
reliance), two clear over-reliance traps (the benefit figure and the eligibility ruling), and
middle-gradation items.

## 6. Content — the ANSWER KEY (SME-reviewed)

> Authoring bar (`docs/content-guide.md`): no AI-isms; plain, realistic civic-tech context. The
> items are **deliberately low on hard factual claims** — the point is the verification *posture*,
> not the output's correctness — so **no specific regulatory facts are asserted as true** (the
> lone citation, item 6, is attributed to the tool's output, not stated as fact). Targets were
> reviewed by an SME pass for defensibility; adjacent postures noted where reasonable.

**Scale (ordered most-trusting → least-trusting):** `use-as-is` → `light-check` →
`verify-key-claims` → `verify-everything` → `dont-rely`.

| # | id | Task (same tool) | Target | Rationale |
|---|----|------------------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | `format-list` | Reformat a list you wrote — bullets/spacing — changing no wording. | **use-as-is** | No factual risk (your own words, formatting only). **Over-verifying here is the under-reliance failure.** |
| 2 | `recap-decision` | Write a short team-chat message recapping a decision your team already made. | **light-check** | Low stakes/internal, but it's now generating content that could misstate the decision — a quick skim is the right level. |
| 3 | `condense-manual` | Condense a long state-policy-manual section into key points for your team. | **verify-key-claims** | Summaries drop conditions / invent thresholds; check the load-bearing points (an internal digest, not every clause). |
| 4 | `draft-faq` | Draft a first-pass public FAQ about a benefit program that you'll edit before publishing. | **verify-key-claims** | Public-facing tempts verify-everything, but it's a first draft with a human edit ahead — verify load-bearing facts now. (The "first pass / you'll edit" framing is what holds it at key-claims, not everything.) |
| 5 | `benefit-figure` | Compute a household's **exact monthly benefit amount** for an eligibility determination notice. | **verify-everything** | Case-specific arithmetic against rules is outside the tool's reliable zone and drives an official determination; usable only if every input is independently re-derived against the source of record. *(SME-noted adjacent: `dont-rely` if policy forbids general AI for determinations; kept at verify-everything for clean contrast with item 6.)* |
| 6 | `eligibility-ruling` | Asked whether an unusual immigration-status mix qualifies; it returns a **confident yes/no with a statutory citation**. | **dont-rely** | The canonical over-reliance trap: confident tone + citation read as authority, but confidence doesn't track correctness on rare edge cases. Escalate to policy/legal — don't act on the model's ruling. |

**Summary-card definitions (≈6th-grade):**
- **Over-reliance (trusting too much):** "You trusted the AI on something it can get wrong and didn't check enough." *On high-stakes work this is the costlier miss.*
- **Under-reliance (checking too much):** "You double-checked work the AI had right and spent effort you didn't need to."
- Framing line: "Good judgment means matching how much you check to how often the tool is actually wrong for that task."

**Framing grounded in:** automation bias (Mosier & Skitka, https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/154193129604000413) and the Jagged Technological Frontier (Dell'Acqua et al., https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838) — both already cited in the 2.8 lesson. `status='in_review'` pending SME sign-off.
