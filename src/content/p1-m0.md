# Mission Briefing: AI at Nava

Welcome to the **Nava Local AI Academy** — a four-week, hands-on sprint designed to turn every member of the team into a confident, responsible AI practitioner. This isn't a vendor demo or a passive slide deck. It's a structured program built around the specific constraints, values, and mission of a govtech company.

> **Core principle:** AI is a co-pilot, not a decision-maker. In high-stakes civic contexts — eligibility, benefits, healthcare — a human must remain accountable for every outcome.

---

## Why This Exists

The public sector is at an inflection point. Agencies are under enormous pressure to adopt AI quickly, yet the populations they serve — Medicaid recipients, SNAP applicants, veterans navigating benefits — are among the most vulnerable in the country. A single bad automation decision can delay someone's insulin, terminate housing assistance, or incorrectly flag a child welfare case.

Nava sits at the intersection of those pressures. We build simple, effective, equitable government services. AI can help us do that work better — but only if we understand exactly what these tools are, where they fail, and how to deploy them safely.

This course exists because **hype-driven AI adoption is a form of risk**. Teams that blindly trust model outputs, or that send sensitive data to cloud APIs without thinking, create liability for clients and harm for the people those systems serve.

---

## The "Local First" Philosophy

**Local First** is the organizing principle of this entire program. It means:

- **Sensitive data must never touch the public cloud.** When a model runs on your machine, data never leaves your machine. No API call. No remote server log. No third-party terms of service.
- **You bring the model to the data, not the data to the model.** This is the inversion that makes local AI safe for govtech work.
- **Capability does not require cloud.** Modern local models (7B–70B parameter range) running via Ollama or LM Studio can handle summarization, classification, drafting, and analysis with quality that rivals commercial APIs — on commodity hardware.

This matters because Nava regularly works with data subject to HIPAA, FISMA, and state-level privacy statutes. "Enterprise" agreements with cloud vendors reduce risk but do not eliminate it. Local inference eliminates the data-exfiltration vector entirely.

---

## Why AI Matters in Public Benefit Delivery

Consider the scale: the U.S. Medicaid program serves over 90 million people. SNAP covers roughly 42 million. Each of those programs runs on a tangle of federal rules, state amendments, county-level waivers, and annual policy updates.

AI — used responsibly — can help:

- **Policy analysts** parse hundreds of pages of regulation changes in minutes rather than weeks
- **Engineers** write and review code against complex eligibility logic
- **Researchers** synthesize user interview transcripts without manually reading every line
- **Operations staff** draft communications that are plain-language, accurate, and consistent

None of those use cases require sending a claimant's Social Security Number to a cloud server. That's the Local First insight: **the high-value work and the high-risk data are usually separable**.

## The 4 Phases of This Course

This academy is structured as a progressive sprint. Each phase builds on the last.

### Phase 1 — Crawl: Mental Models
*You are here.* Before touching a model, you need the right conceptual vocabulary. What is an LLM? What is a token? Why do models hallucinate? What is a privacy architecture? What does ethics look like in practice for civic AI? These four modules ground every practical decision you'll make in later phases.

### Phase 2 — Walk: Interaction Skills
You learn to interact with AI effectively. Prompt engineering, grounding a model on a real policy document, building a system prompt that constrains model behavior, and evaluating output quality. You'll work directly with your local model in every exercise.

### Phase 3 — Run: Local Infrastructure
Setup and operate your own local AI environment. Install Ollama and LM Studio, pull and compare open-weight models, connect a local model to a document corpus, and understand hardware tradeoffs. By the end you'll have a working local AI lab on your machine.

### Phase 4 — Sprint: Specialization
Apply what you've built to Nava-relevant use cases: policy analysis assistants, code review tools, meeting summarization, and structured data extraction from government forms. You'll also learn when *not* to use AI and how to document that judgment for clients.

## Data Sovereignty in a Government Contract Context

**Data sovereignty** is the principle that data is subject to the laws and governance of the nation or entity where it originates. For government contracts, this has concrete implications:

- Federal data processed on commercial cloud infrastructure must comply with FedRAMP authorization boundaries
- State Medicaid data may be subject to state privacy statutes that are stricter than federal minimums
- Claimant PII (names, SSNs, dates of birth, addresses) and PHI (health diagnoses, treatment records) carry explicit legal protection under HIPAA and related frameworks

When Nava deploys an AI capability on a government program, the client agency is the **data owner**. We are a steward. Our obligation is to ensure that every AI interaction with that data has a clear, documented justification and a clear, documented privacy boundary.

Local inference is one of the most defensible ways to honor that obligation. A model that runs entirely on the agency's or contractor's hardware, processes data in memory, and writes no external logs is architecturally aligned with data sovereignty — regardless of what agreements exist with cloud vendors.

## The Nava Mission as AI Guardrail

Nava's mission is to make government services simple, effective, and equitable. These three words are not decoration — they are evaluation criteria for every AI use case:

- **Simple:** Does this AI tool make the experience clearer for end users, or does it add opaque complexity?
- **Effective:** Does it actually improve outcomes, or does it just feel impressive?
- **Equitable:** Does it work equally well across race, income level, language, disability status, and geography?

If an AI use case fails any of those three criteria, it doesn't belong in a Nava product. This course will give you the technical vocabulary to make that argument — and to build tools that pass it.

## Responsible vs. Hype-Driven Adoption

Not all AI adoption is responsible AI adoption. Hype-driven adoption looks like this: a new tool receives press coverage, leadership wants to show it is "doing something with AI," and a team pilots it without clear criteria for success, failure, or harm. The tool is measured by whether it generates positive reactions in a demo, not by whether it produces equitable outcomes in production.

Responsible AI adoption looks different:

- **Define the problem first.** What specific task is slow, error-prone, or inaccessible? What outcome would improvement look like?
- **Identify the data involved.** Is it Tier 1, 2, or 3? Which architecture is appropriate?
- **Define failure modes.** What happens when the AI is wrong? Who bears the cost?
- **Set measurable criteria.** Accuracy, fairness metrics, latency, user trust — pick the ones that matter for your context.
- **Build in human review.** Especially for anything that affects benefit eligibility, case status, or individual rights.
- **Evaluate continuously.** A model that passes fairness checks at launch can drift over time as inputs change.

Nava has a specific responsibility here: our clients are government agencies, and the people affected by our software decisions often have no alternative service provider to switch to. That raises the ethical bar significantly.

## Nava Labs: Where This Research Comes From

Nava Labs is a philanthropically-funded initiative that prototypes and rigorously tests AI tools in real government service delivery contexts. The research underpinning this curriculum is not hypothetical — it comes from live deployments with real caseworkers and benefit navigators.

### The Scale of the Problem

**$227 billion in public benefits go unclaimed every year** in the United States. Eligible families don't receive SNAP, Medicaid, WIC, or housing assistance — not because they don't qualify, but because the systems for accessing those benefits are complex, fragmented, and difficult to navigate. Nava's AI work is directly aimed at reducing that gap.

### Proven Results from the Field

In a rigorous study of an AI assistive chatbot deployed with caseworkers across six Los Angeles County organizations:

- **40% improvement** in caseworker accuracy on complex benefits questions
- **65% of caseworkers** actively used the tool over a 14-week pilot
- Strongest results came from organizations that provided structured training and peer support — technology alone wasn't enough

These numbers are not from a demo environment. They are from a randomized controlled trial with 125 caseworkers, followed by a real-world deployment with 61 workers across LA County.

### The Caseworker Empowerment Toolkit

Nava Labs has developed and field-tested six AI-powered tools for benefit navigators and caseworkers:

1. **Assistive Chatbot** — links families to public benefits using policy-grounded answers
2. **Form-Filling Assistant** — completes benefit applications with caseworker oversight
3. **Referral Generator** — identifies health and social services matched to client needs
4. **Document Analyzer** — validates document compliance (income verification, ID, etc.)
5. **Call Notes Summarizer** — transcribes calls and identifies next steps automatically
6. **Work Requirements Checker** — verifies work-requirement evidence for SNAP and Medicaid

Every tool in this toolkit was built with human review as a structural requirement — not an afterthought.
