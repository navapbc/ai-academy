# Deep Dive: Privacy First

Understanding when and where to run AI is not just a technical question — it is a legal, ethical, and contractual one. This module covers Nava's framework for making those decisions, the nature of sensitive government data, and practical techniques for protecting it when AI is involved.

---

## The Nava AI Decision Matrix

The Decision Matrix is the authoritative guide for determining which AI tools are appropriate for a given task. Every Nava employee working with AI should be able to apply this framework without looking it up.

### Tier 1 — Public Data
**Cloud AI is acceptable.**

Data that is already publicly available or contains no sensitive information. Examples:
- Summarizing a public legislative hearing transcript
- Analyzing published federal rulemaking documents
- Researching publicly available program eligibility rules

Any major cloud AI tool may be used here with normal professional judgment.

### Tier 2 — Internal or Operational Data
**Enterprise cloud AI only, with a signed Data Processing Agreement (DPA).**

Internal Nava data or program operational data that is not publicly available but does not include claimant PII. Examples:
- Summarizing internal Slack threads or meeting notes
- Drafting internal project documentation
- Analyzing anonymized program metrics

A signed DPA ensures the vendor contractually cannot use your data for model training and has appropriate security controls. Do not use consumer AI products (free tiers, personal accounts) for Tier 2 data.

### Tier 3 — Client or Claimant PII / PHI
**Local AI only. No exceptions.**

Any data that identifies or could identify an individual — names, Social Security Numbers, dates of birth, addresses, phone numbers, health records, case details, immigration status, or financial records. Examples:
- Reviewing a claimant's benefits application
- Summarizing case notes with identifying details
- Drafting correspondence that references an individual's record

> **This is the hard line.** Sending claimant PII to any cloud AI service — even an enterprise-tier product — requires explicit approval from program leadership and legal. When in doubt, run it locally.

### Tier 4 — Classified or Restricted Government Data
**No AI tooling without explicit program authorization.**

Data governed by strict federal data handling requirements (CUI, controlled unclassified information, or above). Nava does not use AI on this tier without a formal program-level security review and written authorization.

---

## Why the Tiers Exist

The tiers are not arbitrary policy — they reflect real legal and contractual obligations:

- **FISMA / FedRAMP**: Federal programs require that data remain within authorized system boundaries. Most cloud AI services are not FedRAMP authorized.
- **Client contracts**: Government contracts often include explicit data handling requirements — including where data may be processed and stored.
- **Legal liability**: A PII breach involving a third-party AI service can trigger mandatory breach notification, regulatory penalties, and contract termination.
- **Public trust**: The people Nava serves — benefits claimants, veterans, patients — have a reasonable expectation that their records are handled with care. Violating that is not just a legal problem; it is a mission failure.

## PII Taxonomy: Know What You Are Handling

**Personally Identifiable Information (PII)** is any data that can be used to identify a specific individual. There are two categories:

**Direct identifiers** — unambiguously identify a person on their own:
- Full name
- Social Security Number (SSN)
- Date of birth
- Home address
- Phone number
- Email address
- Driver's license or state ID number
- Medical record number

**Quasi-identifiers** — individually innocuous, but dangerous in combination:
- ZIP code
- Age or age range
- Gender
- Race or ethnicity
- Employer
- Occupation

> **The re-identification risk**: A ZIP code + age + gender combination can uniquely identify up to 87% of the U.S. population according to research by Latanya Sweeney. "Anonymized" data that retains quasi-identifiers is not truly anonymous.

**Protected Health Information (PHI)** is a subset of PII governed by HIPAA — any health or treatment information linked to an identified or identifiable individual.

## The Redaction Bridge

Sometimes you need the analytical power of a frontier cloud model on a document that contains sensitive structure — but the raw document itself includes PII. The **Redaction Bridge** is the technique for handling this responsibly.

### Step 1: Strip Direct Identifiers

Replace all direct identifiers with generic placeholders before the document ever leaves your machine:

```
Original: "John Martinez, SSN 123-45-6789, applied on 03/15/1987"
Redacted: "PERSON_A, SSN XXX-XX-XXXX, applied on [DATE_REDACTED]"
```

### Step 2: Generalize Quasi-Identifiers

Reduce the specificity of quasi-identifiers to eliminate re-identification risk:

```
Original: "34-year-old woman living in ZIP code 97201"
Redacted: "adult in their 30s, Pacific Northwest region"
```

### Step 3: Validate Before Sending

Do a second pass — either manually or with a local model — to confirm no identifiers slipped through before sending to any external service.

### Step 4: Treat the Redacted Output Carefully

Even redacted data can be sensitive. The structural pattern of a benefits denial, a medical diagnosis, or a criminal case can be revealing even with names removed. Apply professional judgment about whether even the redacted version is appropriate to share.

> **The Redaction Bridge is a bridge of last resort** — not a routine workflow. If you find yourself redacting frequently, that is a signal the task should be re-evaluated for local AI.

## Audit Logging for AI

Every AI-assisted action touching Tier 3 data should produce an audit trail. Minimum required fields:

| Field | Example |
|-------|---------|
| Who | Employee name / ID |
| What model | `llama3:8b` via Ollama |
| What prompt | Stored hash or full text |
| What output | Stored hash or full text |
| When | ISO 8601 timestamp |
| Outcome | Reviewed / flagged / discarded |

Audit logs serve two purposes: they support incident investigation if something goes wrong, and they demonstrate due diligence in program reviews and audits.

## Air-Gapped Deployment

For the highest-security environments, models can be deployed on machines with **no network access at all**. An air-gapped deployment means:

- The model files are loaded once onto isolated hardware
- No API calls, no telemetry, no update checks
- Physical security controls govern access to the machine
- Audit logs are written to local storage

Ollama supports fully offline operation once a model is pulled. For air-gapped environments, models are transferred via approved removable media following program security procedures.

## Incident Response: When PII Is Accidentally Sent to Cloud AI

Despite best intentions, mistakes happen. If you realize PII was sent to a cloud AI service:

1. **Stop immediately** — do not continue the session or expand the scope of data shared
2. **Document the scope** — what data was sent, to which service, at what time
3. **Notify your PM and tech lead** within the same business day
4. **Loop in Nava legal** — they will assess breach notification obligations based on the contract and data type
5. **Preserve evidence** — screenshots, session IDs, API call logs if available
6. **Do not self-remediate by deleting** — follow legal's guidance on retention

The goal is accurate accounting, not blame. Early reporting limits liability for Nava and the program.

## Relevant Standards and Frameworks

- **NIST SP 800-188**: Federal guidelines on de-identification of government datasets — the technical standard for what constitutes sufficient anonymization
- **NIST AI RMF** (AI Risk Management Framework): A voluntary framework for identifying, assessing, and managing AI risks across an organization's AI lifecycle
- **HIPAA Safe Harbor**: Defines 18 specific identifiers that must be removed for health data to be considered de-identified
- **OMB M-24-10**: Current federal guidance on responsible AI acquisition and use in government programs

## The Mindset Shift

The wrong question: *"Can I use AI here?"*

The right question: *"Where should this AI run?"*

Every task has an appropriate AI tier. The job of a Nava technologist is not to avoid AI — it is to deploy AI in the right place, with the right controls, in service of the people the programs are designed to help.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The four-tier Decision Matrix is the authoritative guide: public → enterprise cloud → local only → no AI
- PII includes both direct identifiers and quasi-identifiers that can re-identify when combined
- The Redaction Bridge is valid but is a last resort, not a routine workaround
- Audit logs are required for any AI action touching Tier 3 data
- Incident response is about accurate reporting, not self-remediation — notify PM and legal immediately
- The core mindset: *where* should this AI run, not *whether* AI can be used
